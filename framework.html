THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS / CCF

The architecture of coherent consciousness.

The Coherent Consciousness Framework explores consciousness as an emergent information-dynamic phenomenon shaped by interacting modes, nonlinear relationships, and integrative processes.

Explore the core constructs ↗

CCF is a theoretical framework under development. Its proposed mechanisms and measures require further formal and empirical evaluation.

01 / CORE CONSTRUCTS

Three fundamental modes.

CCF proposes three fundamental modes of consciousness. These are conceptual and dynamical categories, not distinct brain regions or anatomical structures.

01

PMC

Physical Mode of Consciousness

The physical dimension of the framework, concerned with physical processes and their role in the dynamics of a conscious system.

02

EMC

Entropic Mode of Consciousness

The entropic dimension, concerned with variability, uncertainty, and the changing informational conditions through which a system evolves.

03

IMC

Integrative Mode of Consciousness

The integrative dimension, concerned with relationships among components and the possible emergence of coordinated, coherent organization.

How to interpret the modes

The three modes are proposed as interacting dynamical tendencies within a system. Their significance lies not only in their individual characteristics, but also in how their relationships may change over time.

02 / EMERGENT CONFIGURATION

CCS: Coherent Consciousness State.

In CCF, coherence is considered in terms of how a system's interacting modes may become organized into a more integrated configuration.

Coherent Consciousness State

A Coherent Consciousness State (CCS) is an emergent state or configuration within the framework. It is distinct from PMC, EMC, and IMC, which remain the three proposed fundamental modes.

A question of organization

CCF asks how relationships among the modes might support coherent organization, how such configurations may arise, and under what conditions they may persist or change. These questions require further formalization and testing.

03 / INFORMATION DYNAMICS

From interaction to integration.

CCF uses information-dynamic concepts to describe possible relationships between the modes and the emergence of coherent configurations.

A

IIF

Integrative Information Flow

A proposed information-geometric framework for describing interactions and transitions among the modes. Its geometry is intended to help characterize relationships and conceptual transition costs.

B

Attractors

Dynamic organization

Dynamical systems concepts provide a way to describe recurring configurations and the conditions under which system behavior may move between different states.

C

Nonlinear dynamics

Interactions and transitions

The framework explores whether nonlinear interactions can account for changes in system organization and the emergence of integrative states under particular conditions.

From concept to formal model

These constructs provide a proposed conceptual architecture. Their mathematical relationships, boundary conditions, stability properties, and empirical interpretations must be assessed explicitly rather than assumed from the terminology.

04 / PROPOSED OBSERVABLES

IMS: Integrative Micro-Signals.

IMS is proposed as a multidimensional way to investigate measurable patterns that might correspond to the information dynamics described by CCF.

Candidate dimensions

  • Modal dominance: relative contributions of P, E, and I.
  • Curvature-related metric: a proposed measure represented by κ.
  • Integration index: Φ*, as defined within CCF.
  • Temporal coherence: a proposed measure represented by τ.

Empirical questions

Candidate approaches may include neural recordings, measures of signal variability, and measures of coordination across time or systems. Each proposed observable needs a clear operational definition, measurement procedure, and validation strategy.

The CCF integration index Φ* is framework-specific and should not be confused with the Φ notation used in Integrated Information Theory.

05 / SUMMARY

One framework. Three modes. An emergent state.

The Coherent Consciousness Framework proposes that consciousness can be investigated through interactions among PMC, EMC, and IMC; that CCS describes an emergent coherent configuration rather than a fourth fundamental mode; and that information dynamics may provide a formal language for studying their relationships.

The framework's value will depend on conceptual clarity, mathematical consistency, testable predictions, and evidence that can distinguish its proposals from competing explanations.
